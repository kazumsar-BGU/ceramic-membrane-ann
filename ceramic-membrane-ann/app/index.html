Identical conditions. All five models receive the same feature matrix, the same folds, the same fold-internal imputer and scaler, and the same hyperparameter-selection procedure (inner 3-fold search on the training split only). No model gets a pipeline the others do not.

Leakage control. Missing values (NR), the min–max scaler, and every hyperparameter are fitted inside the training split of each fold. The held-out rows enter only at scoring time. Feature importance is likewise measured on held-out rows.

Outer folds score the model; inner folds pick its hyperparameters. The outer test rows influence neither.

Leave-One-Paper-Out Validation (pooled predictions across 18 held-out papers)

Model MAE (µm) RMSE (µm) R²

Each paper is held out once and predicted by a model trained on the other 17 — including that model's imputation means, scaler and hyperparameters, all re-selected from those 17 papers alone. The inner selection folds are themselves grouped by paper, so selection is optimised for cross-paper transfer rather than within-paper interpolation.

Every grid is searched by the same rule: inner 3-fold split of the outer training rows, lowest mean MAE (µm) wins, ties broken toward the simpler setting. Nothing here was chosen by looking at the reported CV or LOPO numbers. Where a model's selection varies across folds, that instability is itself a result worth reporting.
The ANN's 6 candidates are the smallest tuned search here — Gradient Boosting explores 20 — so no result can be attributed to the proposed model having been given a wider search than its baselines. Linear regression is the exception in the other direction: OLS has no hyperparameters to search.
